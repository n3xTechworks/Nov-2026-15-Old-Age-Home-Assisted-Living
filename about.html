Our story, our people and the promises we keep

Family-run since 1994, Thymewood has grown from one Victorian house into a garden home for 64 people, without losing the feel of a home.

Our story

It began with one house and a promise to Grandma Ivy

In 1994 Ruth and George Marlowe opened the doors of a Victorian house on Orchard Lane so that Ruth's mother, Ivy, could be cared for close to home, in a place that still felt like hers. Eight of Ivy's neighbours soon joined her.

Thirty-two years on, their daughter Elena runs Thymewood. The house has grown to 64 suites around two acres of walled gardens, but the promise has not changed: every resident is known by name, by history and by the way they take their tea.

  • Still family-owned, with no investors and no chain
  • Half of our carers have been with us for over eight years

Why we stayed small

Couples stay together, and friendships have room to grow

We have turned down every offer to expand beyond 64 residents. A smaller house means husbands and wives can share a suite, carers have time to sit and read with someone, and nobody becomes a room number.

Mission and values

Four promises we make to every family

Our mission is simple: to help older people live well, on their own terms, in a home full of warmth. These are the promises our whole team signs up to.

  1. I

    Dignity before routine

    We fit care around each person's habits, from the time they wake to the way they like their hair done, rather than around a rota.

  2. II

    The same familiar faces

    Each resident has a key carer and a small team who know them well. We do not use agency staff for personal care.

  3. III

    Families are partners

    Open visiting, a seat at the table and a monthly letter from the key carer, so you are never left wondering how your mum is.

  4. IV

    A life worth getting up for

    Gardens, music, outings and real jobs to do, because purpose and friendship matter as much as medication.

Milestones

Thirty-two years of growing, slowly and carefully

Every change we have made started with a resident or a family asking us for something better.

  1. 1994

    The first house opens

    Ruth and George Marlowe welcome nine residents to the Victorian house on Orchard Lane.

  2. 2003

    The walled garden is restored

    Residents and volunteers bring the old kitchen garden back to life, with raised beds at chair height.

  3. 2009

    Nursing care, around the clock

    Registered nurses join the team day and night, so residents can stay with us as their needs change.

  4. 2015

    The Willow memory care wing

    A calm, secure wing for 16 people living with dementia opens, with its own sensory garden.

  5. 2021

    Elena Marlowe takes the lead

    The founders' daughter becomes Registered Manager, after twelve years working on the care floor.

  6. 2026

    The new therapy studio

    A physiotherapy studio and hydrotherapy pool open, also used by our day club guests.

Part of Stamford

Every birthday gets a cake, and the village is invited

Thymewood has always been part of the village. Local schoolchildren visit for reading club, the church choir sings at Christmas, and every resident's birthday is celebrated with a cake from our own kitchen.

  • Summer fete in the walled garden every July
  • Weekly intergenerational reading club
  • Volunteers from the village, from 16 to 86

Standards and accreditation

Inspected, trained and open about how we do

We welcome scrutiny. Our inspection reports and family survey results are available at reception and on request.

Registered and inspected

Registered with the regional care regulator for nursing, residential and dementia care, and rated Good in every area.

Dementia-friendly home

Every carer completes accredited dementia training within their first three months, refreshed every year.

Top food hygiene rating

Our kitchen has held the highest local food hygiene rating at every inspection since 2008.

Living wage employer

Every member of staff earns at least the real living wage, which is one reason our carers stay for years.
